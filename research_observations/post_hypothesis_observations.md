**Observation on Metacognition, Process Extension, and Historical Context:**  
 
Recent research indicates the potential existence of an additional distinct step within adaptive processes—specifically, a metacognitive step. Moreover, this metacognitive component may manifest in multiple variants, each influenced by distinct contextual factors.  
   
Consequently, the original hypothesized cyclical process (**ORDA**: *Observe, Reflect, Decide, Act*) could evolve into **ORDA-M**, explicitly incorporating this metacognitive step. Further still, variants of this expanded process might emerge, represented by alternative notations such as **ORDA-X** or **ORDA-Δ**. These variants would differ based on the specific values of variable factors that define and enrich the metacognitive component (*M*).  

This paper will also investigate the broader nature and evolution of decision-making models, tracing their historical roots back to their formal emergence around the 1940s. It will further explore the possibility of identifying even earlier precursors or conceptual origins that contributed foundational ideas to these models.
